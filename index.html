Why this is not a “health percentage”: smoking affects different diseases differently. There is no scientifically valid way to say that a person is “70% as healthy as a non-smoker” from age, sex, cigarettes/day and years alone. This heat scale is therefore qualitative and educational rather than a clinical health score.

Research resources used for the educational interpretation

• WHO — Health benefits of smoking cessation: describes improvements after quitting, from circulation and lung function to longer-term cardiovascular and cancer risk reduction.

• BMJ — Low cigarette consumption and cardiovascular risk: found that even about 1 cigarette/day is associated with substantial cardiovascular risk; there is no safe level for cardiovascular disease.

• Systematic review — smoking and lung cancer, COPD, IHD and stroke: reports higher risks among current smokers and evidence of dose-response for several outcomes.

• WHO Europe — Effects of tobacco on health: summarizes the broad health effects of tobacco and benefits of cessation.

Important: This calculator provides a general smoking-exposure classification and a qualitative health-impact visualization using current cigarettes/day, duration and age. It is not a medical diagnosis, a validated individual disease-risk calculator, or a percentage comparison with a healthy person. Sex is collected for demographic context and is not assigned arbitrary numerical points.
